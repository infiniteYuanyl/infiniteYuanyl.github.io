Diffusion-based multimodal large language models (dMLLMs) decode by predicting tokens at many masked positions in parallel. Each decoding step is therefore a position-selection problem: the model must decide not only which predictions are reliable on their own, but which positions should be committed together as context for the steps that follow.

Existing confidence-based decoding ranks masked positions independently and commits the top-K, which ignores whether the committed tokens actually contribute complementary visual grounding. We identify a step-level failure of this strategy in multimodal settings: several high-confidence tokens selected in the same step can rest on the same region of the image, which introduces **visual redundancy** among the committed tokens and leaves less complementary grounding available later in decoding.

To quantify the effect we introduce the **Visual Redundancy Index (VRI)**, which measures the overlap in visual grounding among tokens committed in parallel. To control it we propose **Visual-Redundancy-Controlled Decoding (VRCD)**, a training-free, inference-time method that uses token-to-image attention to prioritise visually complementary positions.

Across a range of multimodal benchmarks VRCD lowers both visual redundancy and remaining-position entropy at modest runtime cost. Under longer decoding budgets it yields relative accuracy gains of up to 18.8% on M<sup>3</sup>CoT and 6.9% on MMBench over confidence-based decoding.
