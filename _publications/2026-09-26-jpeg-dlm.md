Continuous diffusion language models commonly assign one latent position to every token, so each sampling step remains expensive even though positions are updated in parallel. Existing two-stage compression methods shorten the latent sequence, but they fix the compressed embedding space before training the generative model. That fixed space can be difficult for diffusion to model and for a decoder to map back to tokens.

We introduce **JPEG-DLM**, which jointly trains a compressor, a flow matching model, and a decoding module. Joint-embedding prediction teaches the model to recover clean target embeddings from corrupted compressed views, shaping an embedding space that is more structured, easier to generate, and reliably decodable.

Across LM1B and OWT, JPEG-DLM achieves the lowest mean Gen-PPL and highest throughput among the compared diffusion and flow models. At a compression rate of 0.5 on OWT, it reaches a Gen-PPL of 34.52 at approximately 2.3x the throughput of ELF.
